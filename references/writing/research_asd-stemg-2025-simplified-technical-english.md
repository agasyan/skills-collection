# ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English
ASD Simplified Technical English Maintenance Group (STEMG). Official site. https://www.asd-ste100.org/ · https://www.asd-europe.org/standards-specifications/simplified-technical-english/
Type: research (standard) · Read: 2026-10-06

## What it says
- **What it is:** "a controlled natural language (CNL) – with clear writing rules and approved words – designed to make technical writing simple, safe, and reliable."
- **Current version:** Issue 9, January 15, 2025; the official copy is free on request. First released in 1986 for aircraft maintenance documentation.
- **Why:** airline readers, "80% … not native English speakers", are "easily confused by complex sentence structures and by the number of meanings and synonyms". "Complex technical instructions can be misunderstood and misunderstandings can lead to accidents."
- **Two parts:** Writing Rules (grammar and style) and a Dictionary of controlled vocabulary.
- **One word, one meaning:** "start" was chosen instead of "begin", "commence", "initiate"; "to fall" means moving down by gravity, not "decrease".
- **Project words allowed:** writers add company or project technical names and technical verbs on top of the dictionary.
- **Sentence caps:** 20 words for procedures, 25 for descriptions. ⚠️ from third-party summaries; the spec itself was not opened.

## For writing docs
- Pick one word per meaning and never swap synonyms.
- Write steps as commands; keep step sentences to 20 words and descriptive ones to 25.
- Keep the domain's own nouns and verbs; simplify the grammar around them.
