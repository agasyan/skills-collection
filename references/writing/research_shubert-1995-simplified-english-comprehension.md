# The Comprehensibility of Simplified English in Procedures
Serena K. Shubert, Jan H. Spyridakis, Heather K. Holmback, Mary B. Coney (1995). Journal of Technical Writing and Communication 25(4). https://doi.org/10.2190/WG69-D74B-4DLL-2WBK (abstract only)
Type: research · Read: 2026-10-06

## What they did
- Experiment on airplane-maintenance procedure documents: Simplified English (SE) vs non-SE, two procedures, native vs non-native English speakers.
- Measured comprehension, finding where content sits, and task completion time.

## Findings
- **Better comprehension where it matters.** "Using SE significantly improves the comprehensibility of more complex documents."
- **Easier to find things.** Readers of complex SE documents "can more easily locate and identify information".
- **No time cost.** SE and non-SE documents "took essentially the same amount of time".
- **Non-native readers gain more.** Scores "appear to be quite different, with the non-native speakers benefiting from SE more"; cell sizes were too uneven to test this statistically.

## For writing docs
- Use controlled, simplified English for complex procedures; the gain grows with complexity.
- Expect the biggest benefit for readers whose first language is not English.
