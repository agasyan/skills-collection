# Not Quite the Average: An Empirical Study of Web Use
Harald Weinreich, Hartmut Obendorf, Eelco Herder, Matthias Mayer (2008). ACM Transactions on the Web 2(1), Article 5. https://doi.org/10.1145/1326561.1326566
Type: research · Read: 2026-10-06

## What they did
- Logged the browsers of 25 experienced web users for 52–195 days each (mean 105) in winter 2004/05: 137,272 page visits.
- Compared each page's stay time with its word and link count, and mapped where users clicked links.

## Findings
- **Most visits are glances.** 25% of pages were on screen under 4 s; 52% of visits lasted under 10 s (median 9.4 s).
- **New pages get little more.** First-time visits: 17% under 4 s, about half under 12 s (median 12.4 s).
- **Length does not buy reading.** Pages left within 12 s still averaged 430 words (all pages: 551); the authors note no one reads that much that fast.
- **Attention sits top-left.** 76.5% of link clicks were in the area visible on load; on non-Google pages, about 45% fell in the upper-left 520 × 400 px.
- **Share of words read.** Nielsen's regression on this dataset (45,237 page views) found 4.4 s more per extra 100 words: users read at most 28% of an average 593-word page, 20% more likely ([NN/g 2008](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-little-do-users-read/)).

## For writing docs
- Assume a 10-second visit: the first screen must say what the page is for and what to do.
- Every extra 100 words buys about 4 s of attention, roughly 18 words read; cut instead of adding.
- Put the key fact, command, or link at the top left, in the area visible on load.
