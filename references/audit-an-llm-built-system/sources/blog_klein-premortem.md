# The Pre-Mortem Method
Gary Klein (2021). Psychology Today, "Seeing What Others Don't" blog. https://www.psychologytoday.com/ie/blog/seeing-what-others-dont/202101/the-pre-mortem-method
Type: blog · Read: 2026-10-06

## What it says
- Klein built the pre-mortem about 30 years earlier to cut project problems at his company; he reports use in boardrooms, wildland firefighting, Army programs and on Wall Street.
- Run it at a kickoff once the team knows the plan: declare that the plan has already failed, then explain why.

## Findings
- **Procedure.** Say an "imaginary, infallible crystal ball" shows the plan "has turned out to be a failure". Each person takes two minutes to write down why it failed.
- **Collection.** Start with the team leader "to set an example of being candid". Each person gives a top reason, the next gives a different one, all go on a whiteboard. The output is "a list of showstoppers".
- **Why it works.** Normal kickoffs carry "silent pressure ... not to surface doubts that might disturb the team's harmony". Here "people show how smart they are by the quality of the issues that they raise".
- **Evidence.** Veinott, Klein and Wiggins (2010): 178 college students judged a plan for an H1N1 outbreak; the pre-mortem group showed "the greatest reduction in (over)confidence, compared to simple critiques, or generating pros and cons."
- **Thin base.** The one study cited is the author's own, on students and a hypothetical plan; the post states no limits.

## For auditing an LLM-built system
- Open the audit session with the people who do the work: "Six months from now nobody uses this tool. Why?" Two minutes, written, before discussion.
- Go round until reasons run out; each reason naming a missing step, a wrong assumption or an unwanted screen becomes an audit item to verify.
- Run a second round with the builders and compare lists; reasons only users raise point at assumptions the build never tested.
