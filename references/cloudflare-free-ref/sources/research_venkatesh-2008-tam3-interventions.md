# Technology Acceptance Model 3 and a Research Agenda on Interventions
Viswanath Venkatesh, Hillol Bala (2008). Decision Sciences 39(2), 273-315. https://zenodo.org/records/895412
Type: research · Read: 2026-10-06

## What it says
- Longitudinal field study of 156 employees at four firms rolling out new systems (two voluntary, two mandatory). Surveys ran after training, then 1 and 3 months after launch; use was tracked to 5 months. Training lasted 4 hours to 2 days; one site replaced paper and DOS records.
- Builds on TAM: people adopt a system when they believe it improves their job (perceived usefulness) and takes little effort (perceived ease of use).

## Findings
- **Usefulness wins.** Perceived usefulness was the strongest predictor of intention to use at every time point.
- **Ease of use fades.** Ease of use predicted intention after training and at 1 month, but not at 3 months; its effect weakens with experience.
- **Anxiety fades, real usability counts.** Computer anxiety mattered less with experience; objective usability and enjoyment shaped ease of use only after hands-on use (1 and 3 months, not right after training).
- **Social pressure counts more under mandates.** What important others think had a stronger effect where use was mandatory and faded with experience, most of all where use was voluntary.
- **Explained variance.** The model explained 52% to 67% of perceived usefulness, 43% to 52% of ease of use, 40% to 53% of intention, and 31% to 36% of use. Plain TAM explains about 40% of intention and use.
- **Proposed interventions.** Not tested in the study. Before launch: design characteristics, user participation, management support, incentive alignment. After launch: training, organizational support, peer support.
- **Incentive alignment (proposed).** Users who see that their use benefits other units, not their own, perceive a lack of alignment, which can lead to low use.

## For internal systems
- Lead with job relevance: show each team, on their own data, the tedious step from their sheet or inbox that the system removes.
- Use training and defaults to carry month one, then judge success by whether the output beats the spreadsheet by month three.
- Involve future users in design, name a peer champion per team, and make sure the people who enter data also get something back.
