# Mental Models and User Experience Design
Megan Chan (2024), updating Jakob Nielsen (2010). Nielsen Norman Group. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mental-models/
Type: research · Read: 2026-10-06
Evidence: examples from live sites and apps; no study data given

## What it says
- A mental model is "what the user believes about the system", not what is true about it. Users build it from experience with other products and update it slowly.

## Findings
- **Jakob's Law.** "Users spend most of their time on websites other than yours," so expectations formed elsewhere decide how people read your design.
- **Models differ by person.** Each model reflects the user's background; the core usability gap is between the designer's detailed model and the user's thinner one.
- **Inertia.** "Stuff that people know well tends to stick, even when it's not helpful." One misconception can derail a whole session: "through failure after failure, they never question their basic assumptions."
- **Broken conventions confuse.** Mobile apps that used Back for hierarchy navigation, and a library site with two search bars, both clashed with established models.
- **Two fixes.** Make the system conform to users' models (card sorting reveals them for navigation), or improve users' models with clearer labels and short explanations.
- **Help gets skipped.** A new concept must be taught despite "users' reluctance to read instructions and help messages"; think-aloud testing exposes the model users actually hold.

## For internal systems
- Treat Excel and Outlook as the "other websites": tables should select, sort, filter, and copy like a sheet; notifications should read like a normal email.
- Spend novelty only where the process requires it, and label fields and statuses with the words staff already use in their sheets and emails.
- Run think-aloud sessions early to hear which spreadsheet and inbox assumptions staff bring to each screen.
