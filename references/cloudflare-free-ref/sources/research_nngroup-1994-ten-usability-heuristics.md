# 10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design
Jakob Nielsen (1994, last reviewed 2024). Nielsen Norman Group. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/
Type: research · Read: 2026-10-06
Evidence: cites a factor analysis of 249 usability problems (Nielsen 1994); the article shows no data

## What it says
- Ten broad rules of thumb, not specific guidelines. Nielsen built them with Rolf Molich in 1990 for heuristic evaluation, then refined them in 1994 from the factor analysis to get "maximum explanatory power." The set has not changed since 1994.

## Findings
- **1. Visibility of system status.** Keep users informed through appropriate feedback within a reasonable time.
- **2. Match the real world.** Use the users' words and concepts, not internal jargon.
- **3. User control and freedom.** Give a clearly marked "emergency exit"; support undo and redo.
- **4. Consistency and standards.** The same word or action always means the same thing; follow platform conventions, since users spend most of their time in other products.
- **5. Error prevention.** Remove error-prone conditions or confirm before commit; prevent high-cost errors first.
- **6. Recognition rather than recall.** Keep options and needed information visible, because short-term memory is limited.
- **7. Flexibility and efficiency.** Shortcuts hidden from novices speed up experts; let users tailor frequent actions.
- **8. Aesthetic and minimalist design.** Every extra unit of information competes with the relevant ones.
- **9. Recover from errors.** Plain-language messages, no error codes, name the problem, suggest a fix.
- **10. Help and documentation.** Searchable, task-focused, concrete steps, shown in context.

## For internal systems
- Label fields, statuses and buttons with the terms staff already use, not table or column names.
- Give every multi-step flow a Cancel and every destructive action an undo or a confirmation.
- Show the state of each record and each background job (queued, running, failed) on screen.
